01 · Research

Angular momentum in motion

My research asks how charge currents generate spin and orbital currents in quantum materials, how those currents cross interfaces, and how much torque they can exert on a neighbouring magnet.

I build quantitative theory from density functional theory and Wannier-function tight-binding models, then compute linear and nonlinear transport responses with purpose-written GPU codes. Many of these predictions are developed together with experimental spintronics groups and tested against device measurements.

σzxy, σLzxy

Spin and orbital Hall effects

Full Hall conductivity tensors for transition metals, oxides and MXene monolayers, comparing atom-centred and modern-theory orbital moments.

τDL, τFL

Spin–orbit and orbital torques

Edelstein effects and unconventional torques at ferromagnet interfaces with TMDs, Weyl semimetals, kagome metals and altermagnets.

Dab = ∫ f₀ ∂aΩb

Berry curvature and nonlinear Hall

Berry curvature dipoles and higher-order Hall responses in ferroelectric, Janus and low-symmetry van der Waals bilayers.

Tσ(E), TMR

Spin-polarised tunnelling

Non-equilibrium Green's function design of magnetic tunnel junctions and barriers delivering highly spin-polarised current.

λ, ωlog, Tc

Superconductivity

Electron–phonon coupling and Migdal–Eliashberg theory for boride superconductors, and data-driven prediction of Tc.

H = Σ t c†c + U n↑n↓

Correlated electrons

Slave-boson methods for correlated kagome metals and quantum algorithms for fermionic lattice models.

06 · Methods

From Hamiltonian to measurement

A typical project runs through four stages, most of them on high-performance computing systems with custom code at the response-function step.

Stage 1

Electronic structure

Plane-wave DFT with spin–orbit coupling. VASP, Quantum ESPRESSO, OpenMX.

Stage 2

Wannier models

Maximally localised Wannier functions and tight-binding Hamiltonians. Wannier90.

Stage 3

Response theory

Kubo linear and nonlinear responses on dense k-meshes. In-house CuPy/CUDA, WannierBerri.

Stage 4

Transport and phonons

NEGF tunnelling with SIESTA/SMEAGOL; electron–phonon coupling with EPW.

Open-source codes

ORCHID

Multiscale charge and exciton transport in organic semiconductors, OLEDs and organic solar cells. It covers Miller–Abrahams, Marcus and MLJ hopping, kinetic Monte Carlo up to full 3D devices with electrodes, image charges, Coulomb interactions and exciton dynamics, the master equation and extended Gaussian disorder models, and 1D/2D drift–diffusion with transients and impedance. Optics with dipole outcoupling, DFT transfer integrals, Gaussian-process surrogates and Bayesian inversion are included. It runs in parallel with MPI, threads and GPU, and is validated against NIST Sesame and published results.

GitHub · Python/Numba · MIT

[U‖g] → σij, σkij(S, L) · Ehull

SymPhase

Symmetry and phase stability for VASP calculations. From POSCAR, INCAR, OUTCAR and OSZICAR it finds the crystal and magnetic space groups and the non-relativistic spin group, classifying ferromagnets, antiferromagnets and altermagnets with their d-, g- or i-wave spin splitting. It assigns spin-space-group numbers and derives the symmetry-allowed charge, spin and orbital conductivity tensors, including unconventional spin-orbit-torque channels and the components that exist only with spin–orbit coupling; linear spin-wave theory for collinear and non-collinear ferro- and antiferromagnets (with Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction), including incommensurate spirals without supercells and a supercell workflow for skyrmion crystals, conical and distorted spirals, resolves magnon chirality splitting and magnon–phonon coupling; spin-space-group numbers cover non-collinear orders, and finite-temperature hulls include quasi-harmonic and anharmonic free energies. From a list of elements alone it collects the known phases from OQMD or the Materials Project and builds the convex hull for any number of elements, with energies above hull, decompositions, finite-temperature hulls with mixing entropy and phonons, and publication-quality figures. It then writes VASP inputs with a SLURM job array, runs in parallel on many cores or with MPI, and comes with tutorials and quizzes.

GitHub · Python · MIT

Jij, Dij, Ai, ω(q)

magex

Magnetic exchange tensors, Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction, magnetic anisotropy and spin waves from Wannier90 Hamiltonians. Rotationally invariant Green's-function method, MPI-parallel.

GitHub · Python · MIT

risb-wannier

Rotationally invariant slave-boson (Gutzwiller) solver for multi-orbital Hubbard models on Wannier90 Hamiltonians, with approximate charge self-consistency and multi-core parallelism.

GitHub · Python · MIT

The comscope DFT+RISB package updated for Python 3.13, NumPy 2, gfortran 13 and Wannier90 3.1, with fixes to charge self-consistency on multiple MPI ranks and to U-scan continuation.
